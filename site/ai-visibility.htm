Can AI find your website?

Your website might be perfectly secure and still difficult for automated search or AI services to read. Being invisible is not the same as being untrustworthy.

Before you optimise for AI, do an Aletheia Trust Check. Verify who is behind a website and what its claims actually say. Then check whether appropriate crawlers can reach its public content.

The gated-community problem

HTTPS does not hide you from AI. However, misconfigured firewalls, bot rules, DNS, redirects, robots.txt, login walls and empty JavaScript-only pages can shut legitimate crawlers out while ordinary browsers still work.

Five checks worth doing

  1. Identity: Is this the actual site and publisher, rather than a lookalike?
  2. Human access: Does the intended public URL return useful HTML over reliable HTTPS?
  3. Crawler access: What do the real robots.txt, noindex directives and verified server logs show?
  4. Discovery: Are titles, canonicals, sitemaps and internal page links sensible and accurate?
  5. AI results: For relevant questions, do multiple search services cite the page correctly? Nonappearance alone cannot prove distrust.

Search and training are different

OpenAI documents OAI-SearchBot for search visibility and GPTBot for potential model training. These permissions can be set independently. You do not have to sacrifice public-site safety to improve legitimate search access.

What have we checked?

On 20 October 2026, one external web search service could retrieve the Swindon.org.uk homepage and some topic pages. That does not establish whether Fasthosts blocks any particular bot, whether robots.txt or a sitemap is correct, or whether Google/Bing have indexed each page. Owner-side testing remains necessary.
